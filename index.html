Workplace Maps: Mobile Capture

A small experiment with Envoy Workplace Maps

Built in an evening with Claude, simply as a thought exercise and for fun. I have no inside knowledge of Envoy's constraints or priorities, so there are plenty of assumptions in here.

The problem

While exploring Envoy, I spent time in Setting up Workplace Maps. I was impressed that it's self-serve. I had assumed map setup was white-glove work done by Envoy's team. However, I noticed three things:

  1. You need a floor plan to start. Not every workplace has a current one.
  2. Rooms are drawn by hand, corner by corner.
  3. Everything else is placed one at a time: desks, safety equipment, amenities. This grows as customers' needs do, for example specialised equipment in a pharma lab.

Opportunity

How might Envoy shorten the time from "create floor" to a published map, without the result being any worse than one made by hand?

Proposal

Capture the space with a phone. One walk-through gives you both the layout and what's in it.

Two ways to start. On desktop, upload a floor plan or capture the floor with the mobile app.

Walk the floor and the plan draws itself. No floor plan needed. (Sped up.)

Things in the space are picked up and grouped.

Rooms are detected, not drawn.

Review, edit and save.

This isn't a replacement for the existing map editor. It only shortens the first draft. Editing afterwards, especially on desktop, will still be needed.

Happy to demo it live. It needs an iPhone Pro, since it uses the LiDAR sensor.

Assumptions

  1. Is this even a problem? If most customers can get a floor plan easily, a cheaper answer is AI that reads the existing plan and tags rooms and labels. That is easier to ship and may be more accurate. Capturing only wins if floor plans are difficult to acquire, out of date, or don't show what's actually in the space. I'd ask Support and recently onboarded customers first.
  2. Even if it is a problem, is it worth it strategically? Maybe not. I don't see this winning more deals. It's more of a quality-of-life improvement for customers who have already committed, and it could lead to fewer support requests about map setup.
  3. If it is a problem, how might one validate it? Test it with customers who recently set up a map. Was it faster? Was it as accurate? Did they easily understand how to capture a space? Are there certain personas or customer segments that this is more helpful for?
  4. Engineering considerations.
    • Detection is narrow. Only monitors and fire extinguishers were reliable. AEDs and first aid kits failed in testing.
    • One room at a time works. Several rooms in one pass is shaky.
    • Untested: glass walls, large open floors, high ceilings, multiple floors. The sensor reaches about 5 m, and Apple's RoomPlan is built for rooms up to roughly 9 × 9 m.
    • Android and non-LiDAR devices?
  5. Privacy. Workplaces may not want their space recorded. Images should be used only to identify specific asset types, and not stored once the map is created.
  6. Envoy hasn't already pursued this idea.
  7. GTM / Sales / Support. Onboarding could be difficult because of a new interaction that could be considered quite nascent.

Later

  • Re-capture to update. When the floor changes, walk it again and the map updates.
  • Less effort to capture. A handful of photos instead of a walk-through.
  • Zones. The same capture could define zones within a floor.
  • 3D. The capture produces a 3D model. I kept the output 2D on purpose: 3D needs a strong reason to exist, it's more to get wrong, and it's harder to render.

How I built it

Built with Claude Code, using Apple's RoomPlan for the layout and an open-source vision model (YOLO-World) to spot objects.
